A character is what you see as one: an emoji, a flag or an accented letter counts once, however many code points it is built from. A word is anything between spaces with a letter or digit in it; Chinese, Japanese, Thai and other languages written without spaces are split into words by your browser's dictionary. Paragraphs are lines with something on them. Reading assumes 238 words a minute, or 300 characters of Chinese or Japanese, and reading aloud 140 words. A text message (plain SMS, not iMessage or WhatsApp) holds 160 characters from the basic GSM set, or 70 once anything outside it appears, such as an emoji, a curly quote or a non-breaking space; longer ones go as parts of 153 or 67. X counts every link as 23, and emoji and most characters outside European scripts as 2. Bluesky allows 300 characters. Search engines cut a page title at about 60 characters and its meta description at about 155.
